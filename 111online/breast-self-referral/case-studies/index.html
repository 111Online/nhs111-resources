Somerset NHS Foundation Trust

Go live date: 23rd January 2025
Sites: Musgrove and Yeovil

Somerset was the first NHS Trust to introduce the breast self-referral pathway through NHS 111 online, providing eligible patients with a direct digital route into specialist breast diagnostic services without needing to see their GP first. The pilot has provided valuable insights into patient uptake, clinical outcomes, referral activity and the operational requirements of introducing self-referral into existing diagnostic services. Analysis of referral activity indicates that overall demand has remained broadly stable, while an increasing proportion of patients are accessing services through NHS 111 online. The experience and learning from Somerset have helped inform the expansion of the programme to other NHS Trusts. Since the introduction of the pilot, Somerset has seen a shift from traditional GP-mediated referrals towards digital self-referral through NHS 111 online, with overall referral demand remaining broadly stable.

Primary care has played a significant role in supporting this transition, with the majority of patients accessing the self-referral pathway through the GP DirectLink. This highlights the importance of engaging local GP practices in raising awareness of the service and supporting patients to access specialist diagnostic services directly.

You can read more about the pilot on the BBC website.

Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust

Go live date: 26th November 2025
Site: Addenbrooke's

Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust introduced breast self-referral through NHS 111 online in November 2025, providing eligible patients with a direct digital route into specialist breast diagnostic services.

  • “Self-referral is a significant step forward in supporting earlier diagnosis and making the most efficient use of our healthcare resources across systems. It is one of the best things we have implemented in recent years. Working with NHS England was fantastic — the whole process was straightforward, and the pathway integrates seamlessly with our existing NHS referral processes. My advice to other hospitals is simple: give it a go!”

    Operations Manager for Cancer
    Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust

Engaging their GPs

Engagement with local primary care teams formed part of Cambridge's implementation approach, helping to raise awareness of the self-referral pathway and ensure GP practices understood how eligible patients could access breast diagnostic services through NHS 111 online.

Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust

Go live date: 2nd June 2026
Sites: North Manchester and Wythenshawe

Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust adopted a phased approach to introducing breast self-referral through NHS 111 online, initially making the service available to eligible patients within a defined geographical area, with plans to expand coverage over time.

The Trust worked closely with Greater Manchester Cancer Alliance and the NHS 111 online team throughout onboarding, with dedicated local leadership helping to coordinate stakeholders and support implementation.

  • “We are delighted to be among the first units to participate in this project and are excited about the opportunity to improve access and support earlier diagnosis for our communities in Manchester. Throughout the on-boarding process, we formed a strong partnership between Trust colleagues, NHS111 and the Greater Manchester Cancer Alliance, which enabled us to deliver a safe, step-wise launch of online self-referral. We found that having a dedicated local project lead was essential to coordinate stakeholders and maintain momentum. The Cancer Alliance has also been central to supporting the launch and regional communications, while also providing wider system oversight and supporting further expansion. We feel this combination of strong local leadership and Cancer Alliance support will be key to maintaining momentum and embedding the pathway for the long term.”

    Speciality Registrar in Breast Surgery & Cancer Clinical Leadership Fellow
    Manchester Foundation Trust
