FAQs

Find answers to frequently asked questions about NHS 111 online breast self-referral, including clinical safety, operational responsibilities, referral management and the requirements for introducing the service.

Clinical safety

How do we know cancers are not being missed?

The pathway is based on national NHS 111 online clinical triage and NICE NG12 guideline criteria, developed with specialist breast clinicians and supported by a clinical safety case. Ongoing evaluation and data linkage work will provide further insight into long-term outcomes.

What happens if a patient does not meet the self-referral criteria?

Patients are advised to contact their GP or seek the appropriate level of care based on their symptoms. The pathway directs patients to the right service rather than excluding them from care.

Could patients be falsely reassured?

Patients who are ineligible for self-referral are signposted appropriately and not told they do not have cancer. NHS 111 online does not offer a diagnosis.

What happens to patients who start but do not complete the referral?

Some patients choose not to complete the referral. Understanding outcomes for these patients is an area of ongoing evaluation and planned data linkage.

Clinical responsibility

Who is responsible for patients once referred?

Once received by the breast service, patients become part of the Trust’s existing pathway and normal governance arrangements apply.

How are did not attend (DNAs) managed?

DNAs are managed according to existing local Trust policies and processes in the same way as GP referrals. Once a referral has been received by the breast service, the patient enters the Trust's existing pathway and normal clinical governance arrangements apply.

As part of onboarding, we will work through the current process during the AS-IS mapping workshop, including how DNAs, appointment booking, referral triage and safety-netting are currently managed. Any changes required to accommodate NHS 111 online referrals will then be agreed as part of the TO-BE process design and documented within the updated Standard Operating Procedure (SOP). This ensures there is a clear, locally agreed approach before the service goes live.

Demand and activity

Will this increase demand?

Evidence from pilot sites shows overall demand remains stable, with activity shifting from traditional referral routes to digital access.

Does this replace GP referrals?

No. It provides an additional route into the same breast diagnostic service.

When does the Faster Diagnosis Standard (FDS) clock start?

The Faster Diagnosis Standard (FDS) clock starts when the referral is received by the Trust. For NHS 111 online breast self-referrals, this is typically the day the referral lands in the designated Trust mailbox (day of referral). Local triage processes do not alter the FDS start date.

Are NHS 111 online referrals treated as urgent suspected cancer referrals?

Yes. Patients referred through the NHS 111 online breast self-referral pathway have met the agreed clinical criteria for referral into the breast diagnostic service. Once received by the Trust, they are managed through the same breast pathway as other urgent 2 week wait referrals and are subject to the same local operational, clinical and performance management processes.

Funding and benefits

Does this generate cash savings?

Released GP appointments are considered non-cash releasing benefits, increasing capacity rather than returning direct cash savings.

Will there be a separate tariff?

No. Patients receive the same investigations and enter the same breast service.

eRS and future state

Why are referrals not integrated into eRS?

Current referrals are managed through a dedicated mailbox. National work is underway to support future eRS integration.

Is the current process permanent?

No. The programme is operating in a test-and-learn phase and future integration is expected to reduce manual processes.
